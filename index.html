Happy or not? Teach a neural network to read faces

100 inputs (a 10×10 picture) → 8 hidden neurons → 1 output, the same kind of network as Pick Activity and Tic-Tac-Toe. This time there are no rules and no perfect player to copy. You make the training data: pick the faces you think are happy, and the network learns your definition. Then we test it on faces it has never seen.

1 · Pick the happy faces

Press every face you'd call happy. Everything you pick becomes a training example labeled happy; faces you skip become not happy. Pick a handful, not all of them: some happy faces must stay unpicked so we have unseen ones to test on. Arrow keys move between faces, Space or Enter picks.

The math, in one screen 
// forward: 100 pixels → 8 hidden neurons → 1 probability
z1[j] = b1[j] + Σ_i  x[i] · W1[i][j]      a1[j] = max(0, z1[j])      // relu
z2    = b2    + Σ_j a1[j] · W2[j]          p     = 1 / (1 + e^(−z2))    // sigmoid

// loss: binary cross-entropy. y = 1 for happy, 0 for not
loss  = −( y · log p + (1 − y) · log(1 − p) )

// backward: the same "predicted minus correct" line as the other two demos
dz2   = p − y
dW2[j] += a1[j] · dz2          dz1[j] = (z1[j] > 0) ? dz2 · W2[j] : 0
dW1[i][j] += x[i] · dz1[j]     then every weight -= learning rate × its gradient

3 · The exam: faces it has never seen

Finish steps 1 and 2. Every face here is one the network was not trained on, so this is the real measure of whether it learned “happy” or just memorized your picks.

Junk images

These were never in the gallery and are not faces at all. The network has to say something about them; notice that it never says “I don't know.” They are not counted in the accuracy above.

Key: network correct network wrong junk (no right answer). “Truth” is the expression the face generator drew, which may not match your taste.

4 · Draw a face, see what it thinks

Click or drag to paint pixels. This face is only for testing: it never goes into the training set. Arrow keys move, Space toggles.

Where the training faces live: the generator puts eyes around rows 3–4 and the mouth on rows 7–9, and slides faces left or right but never up or down. Draw a smile two rows higher and the network has never seen a mouth on those pixels. A plain fully connected network has no idea that the same shape in a different place is the same thing: every pixel is a separate input. Turn on shifted copies in step 2 and retrain to see how much, and how little, that helps. (Real image networks solve this with convolution: one small filter slid across the whole picture, so a mouth corner is a mouth corner wherever it is.)

Try it: start from a random happy face and erase one corner of the mouth. Draw a mouth with no eyes. Draw just the eyes. Scribble. Where does your definition of happy and the network's come apart?

Things to notice: the network can score 100% on the training faces and still miss on the exam. If you only picked faces with big grins, narrow smiles may fail. Junk gets a confident answer either way. Draw a smile two rows too high and it fails; shifted copies help only a little. And the Show the network picture tells you which pixels each hidden neuron is watching: usually the mouth corners.
